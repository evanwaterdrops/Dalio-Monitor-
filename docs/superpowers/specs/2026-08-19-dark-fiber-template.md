# The Dark Fiber Template — canonical phase structure (Evan's original, captured 2026-08-19)

Faithful capture of the phase architecture from Evan's "Dark Fiber Template" document
(v1, 17 Aug 2026; original was an HTML page — telecom 1996–2004 as a mechanism, mapped
to AI 2023–2026). **Figures in the original were subsequently fact-checked**; the
corrected figures live in `2026-08-19-dark-fiber-fact-check.md` and `src/lib/playbook/themes.json`
carries the corrected versions. This file preserves the phase names, mechanisms, and
"phase tells" — the intellectual structure — as the naming authority for the theme lane.

## Core claim

A capex-financed general-purpose-technology buildout is a **financing structure**, not a
sentiment phenomenon. It migrates through four financing states (cash flow → debt →
off-balance-sheet → vendor/circular) while the physical build continues; the break comes
when the last state can no longer be refinanced — a credit event, observable and dateable.

## Four forces

1. **Competitive non-optionality** — a catalyst makes standing still equivalent to losing;
   capex stops being an NPV decision and becomes defensive.
2. **An unfalsifiable demand number** — one growth assumption becomes load-bearing across
   every business plan and prospectus at once, uncheckable because utilisation isn't disclosed.
3. **Financing migration** — the Minsky sequence at capex speed; each step preserves the
   appearance of the last.
4. **Asset-life mismatch** — financing termed against a projected asset life; if true
   economic life is shorter, earnings are overstated and the reinvestment treadmill never stops.

## The eight phases (canonical names + tells)

| # | Canonical name | Mechanism one-liner | Phase tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The catalyst makes capex non-optional | Entry condition is the removal of the option to abstain, not optimism | Capex guidance revised upward in consecutive quarters without revised return assumptions |
| 2 | The demand number becomes load-bearing | One unauditable extrapolation embedded simultaneously in equity model, bond covenant, capex plan | Whether anyone starts disclosing utilisation (in telecom it arrived only after the defaults) |
| 3 | Self-funded expansion — the hedge phase | Early capacity genuinely scarce, early returns genuinely good; manufactures the evidence for later phases | — |
| 4 | Migration into debt — the speculative phase | Capex outruns operating cash flow; the gap is bonded (capex/OCF → 100%) | Equity returning to the funding mix for former net buyers of their own stock |
| 5 | Off-balance-sheet — reported numbers stop describing the economics | Obligations move where analysts can't size them; the loss lands in unexpected places | Depreciation critique: the mitigant (earnings-led market) and the risk are the same number |
| 6 | Vendor and circular finance — the Ponzi phase | Supplier lends the customer the money to buy the supplier's product; a trap, not primarily a fraud | First material customer-receivable write-down at a vendor or GPU-collateralised lender |
| 7 | The credit turn — which leads the equity turn | Credit priced the telecom collapse before equity, legibly and dateably | Idiosyncratic → systemic: broad HY widening WHILE issuer CDS keeps widening |
| 8 | Liquidation, and the second act | Assets clear at salvage; the surplus is real but captured by users, not financiers | — (sharpest AI divergence: 2–3y GPU life means stranded compute has no second act) |

## Aug-2026 position reading (original)

Phases 1, 3, 4, 5 matched/passed · 2 unresolved · **6 current** ("form matched, degree
early") · 7 beginning, idiosyncratic (two issuers) · 8 not observed.

## Where the map breaks (disanalogy ranking, original)

Buyer quality (much longer fuse — the largest difference) · demand evidence (materially
better: booked revenue exists) · earnings character (better, conditionally — weakens if
the depreciation critique holds) · asset life (worse — no second act) · where the loss
lands (worse — 2007-style illegibility, not 2001) · rate regime (supportive — easing, not
tightening).

## Base-rate discipline (original's own caveats)

Goetzmann: conditional on a doubling, ~10% give back the gains within five years; booms
roughly twice as likely to double again as to halve. Greenwood–Shleifer–You: run-up +
characteristics → two-year crash probability up to ~80%; even crashing run-ups peaked on
average ~6 months after the signal with ~30% further gains. Jordà–Schularick–Taylor:
equity bubbles without credit booms barely deepen recessions; leveraged ones add ~a year
and ~3pp of GDP per capita. One analogue is not a forecast; the template supplies a
conditional hazard structure, not a probability.
