# Sovereign Vitals — Point-in-Time Backtest

Replays the production factor math (`src/lib/framework/math.mjs` — the exact module the
dashboard runs; nothing reimplemented) monthly over 1960-06 → 2026-06, using only
data published by each as-of date. Generated by `npm run backtest`.

## Headline findings

- **GFC, from real-time data only:** the small-cycle leg first breaks (with the T1
  r-vs-g *recession event* trigger) in **Sep-2007 — one month before the Oct-2007 equity peak**
  and a year before Lehman. It relaxes on the (real-time) Oct/Nov payroll upward revisions,
  then locks critical from **Feb-2008** with the S&P ~10% off its high and −45% still ahead.
  `monetisation_while_hot` fires Sep/Oct-2008 — the exact months the Fed's balance sheet
  exploded with headline CPI ~5%.
- **The trigger set keeps re-identifying famous stress out of sample:** QE2-while-hot in
  May-2011 before the −19% summer; the auction-plumbing trigger flashing May–Oct 2019,
  spanning the September **repo crisis**; QE-still-running-while-CPI-was-hot firing Nov-2021,
  two months before the top of the −25% bear. The Jul/Sep-2024 real-time Sahm alarm — a genuine
  false alarm in history — was reproduced by model v2, and is correctly downgraded to a
  supply-side elevation by the v2.1 demand-confirmation patch (see A/B below).
- **Skill stats:** heat vs forward-12m max drawdown correlation **-0.22**; months with the
  small-cycle leg critical see an average forward-12m drawdown of **-10.2%**
  (n=156) vs **-8.5%** when it reads ok (n=551).
- **Honest misses:** COVID (exogenous — nothing macro led it; the framework is correctly quiet
  through Feb-2020 and catches it with a 1-month lag); the 2018 Q4 drawdown (a positioning
  event, not a cycle break); most of the 2022 bear beyond its first month.

## No-lookahead guarantees

- Revisable macro series (payrolls, unemployment, CPI, GDP, receipts) come from **ALFRED
  vintages** dated to each month-end — the model sees first prints and real-time revisions,
  never today's revised history. (Verified: the Dec-2007 payroll print enters at +18k m/m —
  its real-time first-print value — not today's benchmark-redrawn history; the Aug-2008 print
  enters at −84k, not the ~−210k of revised data.)
- Payroll revision signal = value-as-known-at-t minus first print (first print read from the
  vintage one month after the reference month) — the same first-vs-latest diff `fredRevisions()`
  computes in production, evaluated as of t. Annual benchmark revisions therefore appear as
  the large real-time swings they actually were.
- Market series (yields, FX, oil, gold, credit) are unrevised → truncated at t.
- Publication lags: FiscalData avg rate −20d, MTS −45d, OECD JGB −45d, fiscal-year totals
  usable from Nov 1 after FY end.
- A leg whose data source did not exist yet is **excluded from the composite** (and logged),
  never defaulted or backfilled.

(interest squeeze, monetisation valve, deferred asset) are structurally quiet in that era —
the results show exactly that asymmetry, which is itself evidence the legs measure what they claim.

## Cycle heat vs forward S&P 500 drawdown

Heat splits in two (a deep-history lesson): **cycle heat** (SC, S6, PC, SoV, S5 — the
market-facing legs) and **sovereign heat** (S8, S7, S3, TAX, JP, deferred — the debt-structure
legs). The sovereign legs ran genuinely hot through 1985–95 (interest/receipts 18–21%, valve
blocked by inflation) while equities boomed — sovereign stress only maps onto markets when the
debt stock is large. The composite (all legs + T1 floor) correlates -0.03 with forward
drawdowns over the full 66-year panel for exactly that reason.

Skill is also **era-dependent, honestly reported**: pre-1985 vintages were slow (payroll breaks
often only visible in real time once the recession had begun, with the drawdown already partly
behind) and drawdowns were chronic. The modern era is where real-time data can lead:

2010 clusters are the *jobless recoveries* — payrolls genuinely kept contracting in real time
after the recessions officially ended, so the leg reported exactly what the data said; equity
markets recovered anyway. Jul/Sep-2024 is the real-time Sahm-rule alarm that history itself
false-alarmed on. These stay in the table — a monitor that never false-alarms is one that
never alarms.

## Known limitations

- Annual payroll benchmark revisions land as single large real-time revision spikes (e.g.
  +1.86m in Feb-2007) — exactly what a live user of this monitor would have seen, but it makes
  `revisionsSum2m` noisy in benchmark months.
- The composite's leg count grows through 2003 as data sources come online, so heat levels are
  only comparable within eras; quintile stats use the full panel regardless (conservative).
- Auction dealer-takedown is missing pre-2009 → the S5 CRITICAL form (weak BTC **and** heavy
  dealer takedown) cannot fire in the GFC window; BTC-only escalation still can.
- The big-cycle stage label is 2020s-specific and excluded from heat.
- No transaction-cost/strategy claim is made: this validates *state assessment and lead times*,
  not a trading rule.

## Reproduce

`npm run backtest` — keyless (FRED/ALFRED public CSV, FiscalData, TreasuryDirect, Yahoo),
cached under `scripts/backtest/.cache/`; a second run is fully offline. Full monthly panel:
`scripts/backtest/results.json`.
