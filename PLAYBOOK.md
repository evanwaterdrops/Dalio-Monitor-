# The Historical Playbook

The playbook layer answers one question — "which history are we living in" — by
matching today's factor-machine reading against a curated library of past episodes
and showing what each asset class actually returned from that point forward. It is
precedent, not advice: the output is *what happened when the machine looked like
this*, never *what to buy*. Full design is in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-historical-playbook-design.md`; this file is the
plain-language summary plus the point-in-time validation record required by that
spec's guardrail 4.

Two lanes, honestly labeled and never blended into one score:

1. **Cycle analogs (measured)** — auto-matched against the same coarse factor states
   the production backtest already computes (`src/lib/playbook/fingerprint.mjs`,
   `src/lib/playbook/match.mjs`). A fixed set of ~16 episodes (`episodes.json`), each
   with a frozen fingerprint extracted once from `scripts/backtest/results.json` (or
   the coarse century panel for pre-1960 episodes), is scored against the live
   reading via weighted categorical overlap (0–100) and banded strong/moderate/weak.
   No continuous nearest-neighbor, no decimal match percentages in the UI, no
   per-episode tuning of weights.
2. **Theme rhymes (asserted)** — hand-curated structural parallels that don't live in
   the Dalio factor vocabulary (e.g. the Dark Fiber template, telecom 1996–2004 ↔ AI
   capex 2023–26). These carry a "THEME — asserted, not measured" chip; they are
   never matched, scored, or validated the way cycle analogs are.

## Honest limits

- **Small N.** The episode library has 16 entries. Any hit-rate statistic below is
  computed against a leaderboard drawn from that small, hand-curated set — it is not
  a statistically independent sample of "history."
- **Coarse pre-1960 tier.** Pre-1960 episodes (1920–21 deflation, 1929–32 crash,
  1937 tightening, 1942–51 war finance, 1946–48 inflation) only have annual,
  bucketed fingerprints (CPI regime, r−g sign, debt/GDP bucket, drawdown state, long
  rate direction — no legs, no triggers). Because a monthly-tier live reading is only
  comparable to those episodes on 2 of those fields (`cpiRegime`, `longRateDir`), a
  live month that happens to share both buckets with `tightening-1937` or
  `crash-1929` scores a perfect 100 against them almost by construction — the
  applicable-field normalization rewards sparse fingerprints. This floor holds
  **regardless of the strong/moderate band thresholds** (see validation table below);
  fixing it would mean touching per-episode fingerprints or weights, which guardrail
  4 exists to prevent. Treat any `tightening-1937` / `crash-1929` top-analog result
  as coarse-match noise, not high-confidence precedent — the UI's "coarse match" chip
  is the intended mitigation, not a numeric one.
- **Pre-1960 episodes rarely surface for the right reasons.** Because century-panel
  rows going into pre-1960 fingerprints are sparse (annual, not monthly), those
  episodes' fingerprints are themselves coarse approximations of an already-coarse
  regime — they should be read as "this decade rhymes," not "this month rhymes."
- **Oil-tag asymmetry (RULING R5, `fingerprint.mjs`).** The `energy_supply_shock` tag
  is computed differently live vs. extracted: the live side reads a trailing y/y oil
  move, the episode side reads a forward-window-maximized y/y move bounded to the
  episode's own date range. This is deliberate — a symmetric trailing read fails the
  motivating 1973 case (WTI's posted price didn't move until Jan-1974) — and it is
  conservative by construction (tags only ever add score when present on *both*
  sides), but it means a live shock's first weeks can under-tag relative to how the
  same episode reads in hindsight.
- **The matcher is silent by design in quiet regimes.** Below the weak-rhyme
  threshold, episodes render dimmed with no expandable card, and the board is allowed
  to say "no strong rhymes." That silence is a feature, not a data gap — see the
  validation table for how often the matcher actually has nothing actionable to say
  once self-matches are excluded (rare, for reasons documented below).

## Matcher skill stats (point-in-time, 1960–2026)

Generated by `node scripts/playbook/validate-matcher.mjs`, replaying every month in
`scripts/backtest/results.json` (1960-06 → present, 793 months) with the same
no-lookahead discipline as `BACKTEST.md`: build the live-style fingerprint from that
month's factor record, take the top strong/moderate analog that is *not* the episode
containing that month, and check whether the analog's historical 12m asset winner
(from its anchor's `h12` nominal returns) repeated. Baselines: always-S&P, and
trailing-12m-winner-persists.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Months with an actionable (strong/moderate, non-self) top analog | 751 of 793 |
| Top-analog 12m-winner hit rate | 17.0% |
| Baseline: always S&P | 46.6% |
| Baseline: trailing-12m winner persists | 41.1% |
| Top-analog usage | tightening-1937:315 · crash-1929:199 · oil-shock-1973:100 · deflation-1920:71 · crash-1987:26 · downgrade-2011:18 · gfc-2007:11 · covid-2020:6 · gulf-snl-1990:3 · ltcm-1998:2 |

**Where it fails, plainly:** the matcher has an actionable top analog for 94.7% of
months (751/793) — not the near-silent, only-speaks-in-a-crisis behavior the design
intends — because two coarse-tier episodes (`tightening-1937`, `crash-1929`) score a
perfect 100 against most monthly-tier live readings for the structural reason
described above, and no band threshold can exclude a perfect score. On the months it
does speak, its top-analog 12m-winner hit rate (17.0%) is well below both the
always-S&P baseline (46.6%) and the trailing-12m-persistence baseline (41.1%) — the
matcher's regime-analog pick is a *worse* short-run asset-winner predictor than doing
nothing or extrapolating the recent trend. Read this layer as "which history rhymes,"
not as a trading signal.

## Band recalibration (guardrail 4 judgment gate)

At the original bands (`strong: 65, moderate: 50, weak: 30`), 764 of 793 months
(96.3%) had an actionable analog — near-vacuous, per the guardrail's own threshold.
Per the brief, `BANDS` in `src/lib/playbook/match.mjs` was raised once, to the
maximum values the frozen band-unit assertions in `scripts/playbook/test-match.mjs`
still permit (`band(70) === "strong"`, `band(55) === "moderate"`):

```
strong:   65 → 70
moderate: 50 → 55
weak:     30 → 30 (unchanged)
```

Re-running validation after the change moved the actionable count from 764/793 to
751/793 — a small improvement, not a fix. As documented above, roughly 75% of months
(595/793) score exactly 100 against `tightening-1937` or `crash-1929` regardless of
where the thresholds sit, because those episodes expose only 2 comparable fields to
a monthly-tier live fingerprint. No further band adjustment can address this without
touching per-episode fingerprints or weights, which is out of scope for this
guardrail. No unit-test assertion boundary was broken by this change; per-field
weights, per-episode fingerprints, and the scoring function itself were left
untouched.
